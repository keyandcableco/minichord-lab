# Minichord Lab

Small web views that use the [minichord](https://github.com/BenjaminPoilve/minichord) as an interface for music theory.

**Open it:** https://keyandcableco.github.io/minichord-lab/

## Views

- **[Pitch-class clock](clock/)**: every chord as a shape on a clock face, with its chord name, set class, interval vector and symmetry, and whether it's a turn (transposition) or a flip (inversion) of the chord before. Follows the minichord's key for spelling and its 19- and 31-note temperaments.
- **[Tonnetz](tonnetz/)**: each triad lights a triangle on the tone lattice, and the trail follows the fewest P, L and R flips from chord to chord, beside how far the voices actually moved against the smoothest possible voice leading. Seventh chords and other non-triads light their notes and every triangle they fill. Follows 19- and 31-note temperaments with their own fifths and thirds.
- **[Roughness](roughness/)**: Sethares's sensory roughness for each chord as tuned, the same chord in all twelve of the minichord's temperaments (choosing one switches the minichord), every major or minor triad in the current temperament against equal, a bar per chord, and the two-tone dissonance curve with the chord's intervals marked.
- **[Beats](beats/)**: a Lissajous figure of any two voices, still for a pure ratio and rolling when tempered, with each pair's nearest pure ratio, cents off it and beat rate from its closest overtones, the pair in every temperament, and a button to hear it. Uses the minichord's master tuning.
- **[Ear trainer](ear/)**: plays a chord or a three- or four-chord progression in the minichord's key, then checks what you play back on the chord buttons (or answer with an on-screen picker), with the bass note too if asked. Hints say whether the root, the kind of chord or the bass was off.
- **[Chord geometry](geometry/)**: Dmitri Tymoczko's twisted triangular prism of three-note chords, drawn in 3D to drag and turn. Height is transposition and position across is the chord's shape; the trail is the straight line between chords, the smoothest voice leading, with its length in semitones. Four-note chords are shown by their three-note core, and the page says so.
- **[Stage](stage/)**: full-window visuals for projection. Ribbons draws each voice scrolling by, Bloom draws each chord on the pitch-class clock blooming outward as the next arrives, and Constellation turns the Tonnetz into a star field. Rough chords shimmer and sweet ones hold still. Night, chalkboard and daylight palettes; the controls and cursor fade when idle; keys 1–3, F, N and D; a demo loop with real glides.
- **[Harmony chart](harmony/)**: the harmonic box chart from the Chord & Scale Diagram Generator, live. The scale runs across the top one semitone per column, and each row is the chord built on one degree, triads or sevenths, in major and its modes, the minors, Phrygian dominant, harmonic major, Lydian dominant, altered, whole tone, the bebop dominant and Barry Harris's major and minor sixth-diminished scales (whose rows show the sixth chord and the diminished seventh taking turns, inversions and all). It follows the minichord's key signature, so the key change combo redraws it; the chord you play lights its row, and one from outside the key gets its own row with its function: a secondary dominant (V/vi), a chord borrowed from the parallel key (♭VI, iv), or the Neapolitan.
- **[Spellbound](spellbound/)**: a game that trains you to find chords and notes on the minichord quickly, with answers timed. Read and play: Spell it, Numerals, On the staff (engraved in Bravura), Slash chords, Key detective, a mix, Sight reader, an arcade round where chords fall from the top and each one played before it lands is blasted, with levels that add chord types, the modifier's roots, inversions, any slash chord and finally Barry Harris mode (switched on mid-round once the field is clear), getting faster as they go, a starting level to choose, and key bars from level 3: set that key signature with the key change combo before the bar lands and the chords after it are all plain buttons, and Seven chords, which sets the minichord's key signature and has you play the key's seven chords in order, with the signature engraved on the staff. The harp, in "Scale per chord" so the chord you hold sets the scale: Harp hunt, Play by number (tunes in scale degrees), Simon says (a melody that grows by a note each round) and Missing note, plus Chord scales, where the game writes a real scale into the custom scale on the chord you press and you name it. Scale rounds can be answered on the chord buttons: a mode by pressing the major chord whose scale has the same notes, anything else through a keypad lettered C to B. The chromatic harp (address 98): Follow the directions (up a fifth, down a minor third…), Pluck the chord and Build the scale. Alternate layout: Hidden layout (seven secret chords loaded onto the button combinations; find the one asked for by ear and press the spacebar), Odd one out (three chords of a family and an impostor), and Shades of the third (31-EDO's subminor, minor, neutral, major and supermajor triads, to put in order or find by ear). Voice leading: Reshape, where you hold a chord and pluck the harp to turn it into another (C to Am is the G up to A) while the game points the cantus at the voice that has to move, from one-voice moves through two and the bass to aiming the cantus yourself; Smooth moves, checked against the minichord's own voice-leading search, and Which voice moved?, two chords voiced by that search where only one voice moves, to name by ear. Mystery settings: Scale detective, Transpose detective, Temperament taster and Tune up, which turns the minichord into a plain tone in the waveform you pick, with no vibrato, tremolo or effects, so the only beating is the tuning. Scales come in four levels, everyday to exotic (phrygian dominant, Hungarian minor, the Neapolitans, altered, Persian, enigmatic and more). Every game but Temperament taster runs the minichord in equal temperament, and each round sets what it needs (harp mode, chromatic mode, MPE, voice leading, slash voice, the modifier, Barry Harris mode) by itself; all of it goes back when you leave the game that needed it, or at "Put my settings back". Every answer says how to press it on your minichord, and every game works without one through pickers and the piano. A round answered right first time moves on by itself; after a miss the game waits for Next, so there's time to read the answer. Right answers ring a soft chime, with a lighter one for each right step of a path, scale or chord; wrong ones flash the card red with two soft low notes, and a Sounds button turns the sounds off. Every answer, once found or shown, lights up on a piano keyboard (a chord from its root, a slash chord over its bass, a scale to its octave, a note among its scale or chord), and Key detective writes each chord's numeral under it after a wrong guess or the answer. A found scale shows how it's built (its degrees, its whole and half steps, and its notes on the root), and so does a chord you missed first. A pill beside the game's name shows which way the modifier goes, pulses when the game changes it, and says when a question needs it. Every note in the chromatic harp games is spelled the way its prompt spells it. The Key setting can also pick a new key each time and set it on the minichord, so the key's notes are all plain buttons.
- **[String quartet](quartet/)**: the four chord voices as a string quartet, lowest to the cello, then viola, second and first violin, each drawn on the platform with the string it would play on lit and a moving bow. Arco, pizzicato, tremolo, sul ponticello and con sordino; six dynamics; two seatings; a hall; the cello an octave down; parts kept in their ranges. Each voice glides on its own bow with MPE. Below the platform an open score writes the four parts as they play, viola in alto clef and cello in bass, in the minichord's key signature, and carries them off to the left; the minichord's MPE, voice leading, glide, temperament, slash voice and cantus can be set from the page.
- **[Choir](choir/)**: each voice of the chord sung by its own singer, from recorded choirs or synthesized ones, with the minichord's MPE output, voice leading, glide and temperament switched from the page, a pitch trace, the last eight chords in closed score with parallel fifths and octaves marked, a voice-motion readout that names any parallels, an MPE MIDI recorder, a demo of the minichord's own voicings with voice leading off and on, and slash chords: on firmware 13, "slash voice" (bass, tenor, alto or soprano, counted up from the bottom) and "re-voice", with the singing voice's strip marked and each slash drawn on the trace; on older firmware, "slash replaces"; on firmware 14, "cantus", the voice the harp sets. It keeps its own chant-book look, with the Lab's ivory and ebony as its second look.

## Layout

- `core/minichord.js`: the shared connection to the minichord. It reads the chord port over Web MIDI with MPE zones and per-voice bends, so every voice has an exact pitch, and reads the parameter dump over sysex for the key, temperament and MPE settings. Pages listen for `voices`, `chord`, `device`, `status` and `harp` events; `harp` reports each plucked string, from the harp port or the harp channels in single port mode. A `chord` event waits for any glide to finish, since the minichord sends each new note first and then bends the voice home, so views always see the chord as it lands.
- `core/theory.js`: pitch-class sets in any equal division: prime forms, Forte numbers, interval vectors, symmetry, transposition and inversion, and chord names spelled for the key, the smoothest voice leading between two chords, Sethares's roughness, and pure ratios and beat rates.
- `core/temperaments.js`: the minichord's temperaments as whole-cent offsets, generated from `firmware/generator/temperaments.py` on the firmware's `test-allFeatures` branch, so a view can tune any chord in any of them, and helpers that pick the minichord's exact MPE pitches when it sends them.
- `core/sound.js`: a small sampled piano for views that play something themselves, with a synthesized fallback.
- `core/music.css` and `core/fonts/`: subsets of Steinberg's Bravura for music: the treble and bass clefs, whole and black noteheads and accidentals for engraved staves (`lab-music.woff2`), and the text accidentals ♭ ♮ ♯ 𝄫 𝄪 (`lab-accidentals.woff2`, from Bravura Text), which `core/lab.css` puts first in every font stack so chord symbols get real music signs. Both are under the SIL Open Font License and renamed as its Reserved Font Name requires; the licence is in `core/fonts/OFL-lab-music.txt`.
- `core/lab.css`: the shared look, a chalkboard in dark mode and a whiteboard in light.
- One folder per view.
- `samples/`: sound files, the piano for the ear trainer and the recorded choirs, with their sources and licenses in `samples/README.md`.

## Requirements

- Chrome, Edge or Opera on a computer or Android. Safari and iOS browsers have no Web MIDI.
- Allow MIDI access, including system-exclusive messages, so the views can read the key and temperament.
- For MPE, key reporting and the temperaments, the unofficial `test-allFeatures` build from [keyandcableco/minichord](https://github.com/keyandcableco/minichord). Every view has test chords that work without a minichord.

## Running it locally

The views are ES modules, which browsers won't load from a plain file. Serve the folder:

```
python3 -m http.server 8000
```

then visit http://localhost:8000/.
