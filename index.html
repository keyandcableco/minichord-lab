Minichord Lab

Pages that turn the minichord into a way to learn and play with harmony. Each listens to the chord buttons over MIDI and shows what your chord is doing.

The arcade

Full arcade games played on the minichord itself, each on a page of its own to share. Enter the Minichord Arcade →

Chord Invaders

Chords fall through space and your ship shoots each one down when you play it on the buttons. Set the key when a key bar falls.

Harp Command

Missile Command for the harp: notes fall toward twelve cannons, one under each string. Pluck the string that plays a note to shoot it down.

Chord Snake

Snake, steered on the harp as a d-pad: eat notes, and when the ones you carry spell a chord, play it on the buttons to cash them in.

Chord Asteroids

Play a chord asteroid's chord to crack it into its notes, then pluck each note on the harp to shoot it down before it reaches your ship.

Chord Stack

Tetris, where the blocks are notes. Move and rotate the falling pieces to get a chord's notes into a row, then play it on the minichord to clear them. Side by side, they score double.

Chord Breakout

Arkanoid with chords: steer the paddle with a knob on the minichord, crack a brick with the ball, and play its chord before the ball comes back to break it.

Fifths Defender

Tempest on the circle of fifths: turn a knob to aim down a key's spoke, and play that key's chord to fire at what crawls in. Later its relative minor, its dominant seventh, or its note on the harp.

Chopper Rescue

A rescue flight that runs on the radio: decode its coordinates, "survivors at vi in G", into a chord and play it to fly there. Nothing on the map gives it away.

Key Fleet

Battleship on the chord buttons: the enemy ships are keys hidden on the chart, three chords side by side. Fire by playing chords and work out the keys from the hits.

Chord Sweeper

A game of tension and release: each mine is a key's home, and the chords swept around it point there, its V7 beside it, V of V two away, V of V of V three. Find it, then play its chord to resolve it.

Between the Frets

A quarter-tone ear game: a note, then another, the same or a quarter-tone off? Then find the note in between, where the modifier means a quarter-tone.

Sight Line

Sight-reading on the minichord: notes and chords scroll along the staff to a playhead. Pluck the notes on the harp, play the chords on the buttons, follow the key changes and swing the inversions.

Chord Hunt

Duck Hunt by ear: home sounds, then the duck's chord. Name it against home, I to vi to V of V, and play it on the buttons to shoot it down.

Key Racer

Pole Position through the key: the key's chords are fuel and the rest are oil. Steer on the harp, play the gates open, pull rivals over with their chords, and race the circle of fifths against the clock.

Play and practise

The Practice Room, ear training, and the minichord as a choir, a string quartet or a light show.

The Practice Room

Games that make you quick on the minichord: read chords and find them on the buttons, play scales and tunes on the harp, reshape one chord into another, find secret chords by ear, and every arcade game too.

Ear trainer

Hear a chord or a progression in your key, then play it back on the chord buttons. It checks the chord, and the bass note if you like, with hints when you miss.

Choir

Each voice of the chord sung by its own singer, with voice leading, glide and temperament controls and an MPE recorder.

String quartet

The four chord voices played by cello, viola and two violins, each on the string a player would choose, bowed, plucked, trembling, by the bridge or muted.

Stage

Full-screen visuals for a projector at a gig: ribbons, blooms and constellations drawn live from the voices, with rough chords shimmering and sweet ones still.

8b8 Link

The minichord playing the 8-Bit 8asterd's three AY sound chips, on the board or in its emulator: in the minichord's own tuning, its knobs turning the 8b8's settings, its rhythm mode clocked to an 8b8 drum pattern.

See the harmony

What the chords are and how they relate: shapes, maps and charts that fill in as you play.

Harmony chart

Every chord of a key on one chart, one column per semitone, filled in from the key signature you set with the key change combo. Play a chord and its row lights up; a chord from outside the key gets its own row and a name for what it's doing.

Pitch-class clock

Every chord as a shape on a clock face. Transposing turns it, major to minor flips it, and the clock follows 19- and 31-note temperaments.

Tonnetz

Each triad lights a triangle on the map of harmony, and the trail names every P, L and R flip, beside how far your voices moved against the smoothest way.

Chord geometry

Every three-note chord as a point in Tymoczko's twisted prism. Going up transposes, going across changes shape, and the line between two chords is the smoothest voice leading.

Hear the tuning

What the temperaments do to a chord, measured and drawn.

Roughness

How rough each chord sounds in every temperament the minichord knows: one number per chord, the sweet and sour keys, and the dissonance curve behind them.

Beats

Two voices drawn against each other like an oscilloscope: still for a pure ratio, rolling when tempered. How fast every interval beats, in every temperament.

You'll need a minichord with the unofficial test-allFeatures firmware for MPE, key and temperament, and Chrome, Edge or Opera. Every view also has test chords that work without one.
