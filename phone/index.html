Minichord Lab

Phone

Play a song on your phone into the minichord and play along with it. The minichord listens: it pumps your chords with the kick, opens a filter on the hi-hats, locks its rhythm to the beat and strums the harp with it, puts the song through a DJ's filter and echo, and plays a second of it back on the harp strings.

  1. Plug the minichord into the phone (or a computer) with a USB cable. It shows up as a speaker and a MIDI device.
  2. Play a song in any app: Spotify, YouTube, anything. To hear it through the minichord (which the DJ box needs), switch below to play along.
  3. Connect here, then switch on whatever you like. Everything is borrowed and given back when you leave.

How it works

The phone plays to the minichord as if it were a speaker, and the minichord listens to what arrives. Every few milliseconds it measures the song three ways: all of it, the bass below 150 Hz where the kick drum is, and the treble above 6 kHz where the hi-hats are. Each is judged against the song's own loudest moments of the last few seconds, so the phone's volume doesn't matter and a quiet song swings as far as a loud one.

A follower turns one of those levels into a setting: the chords' volume dipping on every kick is the pumping of sidechain compression, and the filter opening on every hi-hat is an auto-wah. The kick is a sudden jump in the bass, and it can strike your chord again or go out as a MIDI note. For the beat, the minichord looks back over six seconds for the spacing at which the music best repeats itself, then lines its rhythm, its MIDI clock and the harp's strum up with it.

The DJ box sits on the song's own path to the speaker: a filter that muffles it one way and thins it the other, an echo timed to the beat it found, and a tape stop that slows the song to a halt as a turntable does when its power goes. The minichord keeps the last second of the song in memory as it plays, which is what the tape stop slows down and what the harp plays when you grab it, at a third of the usual sample rate, gritty in the way early samplers were.
